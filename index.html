Avoid scams

  • Never pay "shipping", "customs", "processing" or "registration" fees for a "free" laptop. Genuine programmes don't ask for money via transfer, gift cards or crypto.
  • Fake government laptop schemes are common — e.g. viral "PM Free Laptop Scheme" messages in India or "free NSFAS laptop" sites in South Africa. Check only official government (.gov / .gov.xx) or university websites.
  • Never share passwords (e.g. your myNSFAS or student portal login), bank details or copies of your passport with strangers.
  • Ignore social media DMs and "giveaways" asking you to like, share and send personal details.
  • If someone donates a laptop, meet in a public place (e.g. your university campus).

About this guide

Why GiveInternet can't send you a laptop

GiveInternet.org only provides laptops and internet through vetted local partner organisations, after a long planning process with each partner. We are not able to respond to individual requests, and we know how frustrating that is. This guide lists the routes that individual students can realistically use themselves, starting with the ones most likely to work.

Caveats

  • Programme details change often. We checked official pages where possible in late 2026, but confirm eligibility on each website before applying. Each programme shows when it was last checked.
  • Some sources disagree: some websites say a US Pell Grant qualifies you for Lifeline, but official and Congressional sources indicate it only does so for certain survivors; it does qualify you for PCs for People.
  • Tamil Nadu's scheme was launched in January 2026 under one state government; check with your college whether distributions are continuing.
  • Globetops' and Screen Share UK's current activity could not be confirmed.
